+++\ntitle = "Reviewing the Jinhao 59: A $7 Pilot Elite 95 Clone?"\ndate = "2026-09-15T14:25:10.770+01:00"\ndraft = false\nslug = "jinhao-59-review"\n+++\n\n![20260915_125257 1.jpg](/images/jinhao-59-review/20260915_125257 1.jpg)\n\n![20260915_125322.jpg](/images/jinhao-59-review/20260915_125322.jpg)\n\n![20260915_125337.jpg](/images/jinhao-59-review/20260915_125337.jpg)\n\n## Introduction\nJinhao has a history of making pens inspired by other manufacturers. The perfect example of that is the Jinhao 80 and the Safari clone. Up until recently, though, they stopped hiding their clones. The Jinhao 10 marked the beginning of this trend. Their recent addition to the lineup, however, truly takes the cake.\n\nIt is visually impossible to see any correlation between the Pilot Elite 95s and the Jinhao 59. The Pilot is already a very unique pen that only a handful of companies have ever attempted to copy. Actually, the only two I can think of are the Platinum and Sailor pocket pens, but they haven't seen much success due to Pilot's dominance and the high price point.\n\nWell, Jinhao may not have market dominance, but it is cheap. For $7 on AliExpress, you get a pen that looks like it's worth $120—the actual cost of the Pilot E95s. And dare I say, after comparing it to the Pilot Vintage Elite and the Platinum Vintage Pocket Pen, I prefer the Jinhao.\n\n## Nibs\nThe golden nibs on the Pilot and Platinum are very bouncy and responsive. They are the most responsive and precise pens I've used, with Pilot being smooth and Platinum having their signature feedback. What does the Jinhao offer? A Lamy nib. I'm biased here because, despite their occasional inconsistency, I love Lamy nibs. They are precise, stiff, and have a hint of feedback—everything I look for in a pen. I'd take these nibs anywhere, gladly. They come in any style you can imagine: hooded, large, small, gold, steel—and they are damn reliable. They are the most reliable pen nibs I have ever encountered.\n\n## Quality Control and Mechanics\nJinhao's quality control is notoriously patchy, but it has improved massively. The specific model I received is slightly off-center by about 2mm near the nib, but otherwise, the build quality is excellent. It's a really well-made pen that uncaps and posts just as smoothly as the originals. It also comes with a converter that accepts international cartridges, which is miles better than the Con-40.\n\nMechanically, the threads are well-made and I have no concerns about the cap. There's no scratching or dents. The only difference between the Pilot/Platinum originals and this pen is the clip. Both originals feature a spring clip, while the Jinhao uses an old-school tension clip. The Jinhao is also just a tiny bit larger overall, and in my experience, feels more comfortable in the hand.\n\n## Performance and Conclusion\nIn my experience, all Jinhaos seem to struggle with nib dry-out. But they dry out much slower here. The inside of the cap features a proper lining that retains ink significantly better than in their other pens. Unfortunately, after about a week of non-use, it still skipped on the first stroke. Do better next time, Jinhao!\n\n## Conclusion\nOverall, the ethics here may be questionable, but there is no denying that Jinhao made a great pen. Better yet, they made it cheap. Every engineer knows that designing an expensive product is easy; making a cheap one, on the other hand, is the true art of engineering.\n\n![20260915_125538.jpg](/images/jinhao-59-review/20260915_125538.jpg)
