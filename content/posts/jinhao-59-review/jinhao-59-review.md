+++\ntitle = "Jinhao 59 Review"\ndate = 2026-09-15T13:49:45+01:00\ndraft = false\nslug = "jinhao-59-review"\n+++\n\n![20260915_1252571.jpg](/images/jinhao-59-review/20260915_1252571.jpg)\n\n![20260915_125322.jpg](/images/jinhao-59-review/20260915_125322.jpg)\n\n![20260915_125337.jpg](/images/jinhao-59-review/20260915_125337.jpg)\n\n## Introduction\n\nJinhao has a history of making pens inspired by other manufacturers. The perfect example of that is the Jinhao 80 and the Safari clone. Up until recently, though, they have stopped hiding their clones. The Jinhao 10 marked the beginning of this. Their recent addition to the lineup, however, takes the cake.\n\nIt is visually impossible to see the correlation between the Pilot Elite 95s and the Jinhao 59. The Pilot is already a very unique pen that only a handful of companies ever even tried to copy. The only two I can think of that actually are the Platinum and Sailor pocket pens, but they haven't seen much success due to Pilot's dominance and the price point.\n\nWell, Jinhao may not have market dominance, but it is cheap. $7 on AliExpress will get you a pen that looks like it costs $120, which is what the Pilot E95s goes for. And dare I say, after comparing it to the Pilot Vintage Elite and Platinum Vintage Pocket Pen, I like the Jinhao the most.\n\n## Nibs\n\nThe golden nibs on the Pilot and Platinum are very bouncy and responsive. They are the most responsive and precise pens that I have seen, with Pilot being smooth and Platinum having their signature feedback. What does the Jinhao offer? A Lamy nib. Here I am biased because, despite their inconsistency, I love Lamy nibs. They are precise, stiff, and have a hint of feedback, all of which I love in a pen. I will take these nibs anywhere, gladly. They can be made in any style, whether it's hooded, big, small, gold, steel, and they are *damn reliable*. They are the most reliable pen nibs that I have ever seen.\n\n## Quality Control and Mechanics\n\nJinhao's quality control is known to be patchy. But it did improve massively. The particular model that I received is off-center by about 2mm at the nib, but other than that the quality is about what you would expect. It's a really well-made pen that's just as nice and smooth to uncap and post as the originals, and comes with a converter that accepts international cartridges and is miles better than the Con-40.\n\nMechanically, the threads are well-made and I have no concerns about the cap. No scratching or dents. The only difference between the Pilot/Platinum and this pen is the clip. Both originals have a spring clip, while the Jinhao has an old-school tension clip. The Jinhao is also just a tiny bit bigger overall and, in my experience, more comfortable in the hand.\n\n## Performance and Conclusion\n\nIn my experience, all Jinhaos seem to have a nib dry-out problem. But this one learns well and quickly. Here the inside of the cap has proper lining and holds ink better than their other pens. But unfortunately, after about a week of not using the pen, it still skips on the first stroke. Do better next time, Jinhao!\n\n## Conclusion\n\nOverall, the ethics here may be questionable, but there is no denying that Jinhao made a great pen. And better yet, they made it cheaper. Every engineer knows that making an expensive product is easy; making a cheap one, on the other hand, is the true art of engineering.\n\n![20260915_125538.jpg](/images/jinhao-59-review/20260915_125538.jpg)
